# WOVB — Work Ownership & Value/Revenue Benchmark: Quant Research Case
Timestamp: 2026-11-26 (Asia/Shanghai; date precision)
Status: proposed model, awaiting practitioner review. WOVB retains the user's original name (Work Ownership Venue Benchmark); "venue" in this context is interpreted as value/revenue, not a trading venue. The four stages are not verified internal promotion or work-allocation rules.

## Four-stage responsibility progression
1. Assisted execution: clean, validate and document authorized data using Python, NumPy, pandas, SQL and optionally SQLAlchemy. Deliver a reproducible dataset, schema, quality report and tests; confirm point-in-time availability, missingness and corporate-action treatment.
2. Module ownership: own a bounded data module, database/pipeline, API or reusable tools. Define interface, lineage, tests, reliability, handoff, access and maintenance responsibilities. Assess time and support requirements.
3. Value/revenue creation: formulate one or two factor hypotheses, implement a simple baseline, evaluate IC, exposures, walk-forward out-of-sample results, costs and turnover. A promising signal is not realized revenue; strategy deployment requires portfolio/risk/execution validation and authorization.
4. Value/revenue scaling: develop multiple differentiated signals, test incremental value and correlations, combine factors, construct portfolios, validate capacity, risk, execution and net performance; scale only after appropriate review. The stack may remain Python/SQL/database at first, but scale may require additional tools and infrastructure. Stages can overlap, differ by team and do not guarantee progression.

## Real-process validation
Professional conversation → job shadowing → executive shadowing where relevant → micro-internship / one-day experience → formal project collaboration. Each requires consent, appropriate access, conflict-of-interest checks and time arrangements. Observation programs (often called insight programs, not necessarily internships) and actual work placements differ; no claim about current Jane Street program dates or eligibility. Seek actual day-to-day examples across junior/senior practitioners, without requesting proprietary data or signals.

# EB — Execution Benchmark v1.0
Timestamp: 2026-11-26 (Asia/Shanghai; date precision)
Status: proposed personal benchmark; not empirically calibrated.
Purpose: turn a thought or WOVB hypothesis into a reviewed, executed and verified outcome quickly, without sacrificing rigor.

## Loop
Idea → define falsifiable claim and smallest next action → rapid evidence collection → practitioner/peer review → revise → execute bounded task → verify delivery and outcome → close or iterate.
Peer review means substantive critique by relevant people; informal practitioner feedback is not formal academic peer review. A review is not proof, and an idea is not a tradable strategy until independently validated.

## Gates
G0: objective, owner, next action, acceptance criteria and deadline recorded.
G1: evidence source, time, reliability, counterexample and unresolved questions recorded.
G2: reviewer feedback and action taken recorded; disclose reviewer unavailability rather than invent review.
G3: actual deliverable produced with permission and reproducibility where applicable.
G4: acceptance criteria checked, outcome documented, status CLOSED / ITERATE / BLOCKED with next action.
Fast execution does not bypass approvals, privacy, risk controls, scientific validation or employment obligations.

## Suggested metrics
Time-to-first-action; time-to-first-evidence; time-to-review; time-to-verified-delivery; closure rate; rework rate; evidence quality; unresolved blocker age. These are proposed indicators, not measured results. Avoid rewarding speed alone or treating pending outreach as closure.
